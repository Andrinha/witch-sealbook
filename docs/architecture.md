# Seal architecture

A seal is taken apart like a small program: three independent parts, each knowing only its own data format. A new sign or mechanic is added as data in the dictionary or as a new rule in one part, without rewriting the others.

```
strokes ──► reading the drawing ──► seal tree ──► meaning ──► spell ──► manifestation ──► Noita projectiles
            seal.lua                 (AST)         seal_spell.lua (spell) cast.lua
```

A drawing that looks like one of the wiki's seals as a whole is named after it (`seal_canon.lua`), read sign by sign or not.

The main reference is the Independent Witch Hat Atelier Wiki (local copy in `../reference/wha-wiki`, the pages Spells, Sigils Explained, Signs Explained). The shapes of the signs are the templates of [wha-spell-simulator](https://github.com/ytnrvdf/wha-spell-simulator) (MIT) and the wiki's drawings.

## Dictionary

- `files/templates.lua` - the shapes of the signs (generated by `tools/import_templates.py`). Element sigils are drawn upright, modifiers in the pose "at the bottom of the ring, facing the center".
- `files/dictionary.lua` - the meaning: elements and their condensed variants, modifiers (parameter deltas, form, directionality, what they do inverted), the table of effects "element × form → Noita projectile" with where it appears. Both the book (`notebook.lua`) and the script of the book in hand (its own Lua context) read it.

## 1. Reading the drawing → seal tree (`parse_seal`)

Geometry only, no magic.

1. The ring is the biggest closed circle; it can be drawn in several arcs, each must lie on the circle. A gap wider than ~14° - the ring is not closed. The notebook reads the seal the moment a stroke closes the ring (`seal_ring_closed`): a short stroke over the gap closes it too, and a closed circle inside a still open ring is a future layer and does not awaken the seal. If recognition fails, each further completed stroke retries recognition while the ring remains closed; idle frames do not retry.
2. The strokes inside the ring are grouped into symbols by closeness. If a group isn't recognized, it is read by its touching parts; if that fails too, the parts farthest from the center are "pinched off" one by one (signs stand farther out than the element sigil) and read separately, and the rest as one symbol: this way a wind sigil of separate rays doesn't stick to the long stems of the signs next to it.
3. A symbol has an angle and distance from the ring's center (as a share of the radius), and a size (as a share of the radius).
4. A symbol is compared with the dictionary using [$P+](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/pdollarplus.pdf): as an element sigil - as drawn; as a modifier - turned into the canonical pose by its place on the ring (center above), against the templates in every pose 15° apart. The best pose is one angle, from which `sign_pose` gets both the inversion (closer to "outwards") and the sideways turn (`turn`), taking the sign's symmetry into account (`symmetry`: for a triangle inversion is 60°, signs with even symmetry have no inversion). The best score wins; an inverted sign needs a margin.

The recognizer keeps 48 uniformly sampled points, with uniform scaling to preserve proportions. Each point has its normalized turning angle, so matching compares both position and local bending. Endpoints of each resampled stroke have angle zero: a pen lift must not invent a corner joining unrelated strokes. The $P+ distance matches every point to its nearest target and then covers unmatched target points in reverse; the smaller of the two directed distances wins. It still does not check a symbol's complete topology or learn new handwriting automatically.

Flickering Light also needs a nearly closed star outline. Up to three strokes are joined by their endpoints in either order or direction, allowing gaps of up to 18% of the symbol's size. Signed turns on the same normalized samples must show at least four substantial inward valleys between rays. Smooth ovals and convex polygons fail this check even if their point clouds resemble the star. If the structure fails, the remaining sigil templates are still considered; the score thresholds for other elements stay the same.

```
{ ring = { x, y, r, roundness }, symbols = { { kind = "sigil"|"sign", key, angle, dist, size, inverted, turn, score } } }
```

The tree is recursive: concentric rings are layers (`layers`), small rings inside are nested seals (`subs`), small rings outside joined to the ring by a line are linked seals (`links`), a big sign around the sigil inside the ring is a frame (`frames`), a band of small marks between rings is windows (`band = "windows"`), "claws" outside are glaives (`glaives`). The sigil is read first: everything in the middle of the ring is read together (or as two sigils side by side), the rest is split into signs - first the touching strokes, then neighboring groups are merged if they read better together (weighing how much of the drawing each part explains). A sign in the very middle of the ring has to read clearly better than two sigils side by side. Strokes under 3 units are blots and are not read (a touch of the pen is a dot); if some sign doesn't read without them, the seal is read again with marks from 2 units (the small strokes of the Carousel of Lights).

The point-cloud score measures resemblance, not the probability that a symbol is correct. The game's score is `max(1 - 4 * distance / 48, 0)`; $P's old weighted distances cannot be substituted into this scale. A score of 0.33 admits a candidate while strokes are grouped. Before a spell is accepted, its mean normalized symbol quality must also reach 0.35, independently of the ring. The mean quality of the element sigils, taken separately from modifiers, must reach 0.65 (about 0.538 raw score for a single sigil): clear modifiers cannot rescue a weak element, while paired elements can tolerate uneven drawing. Qualities are clamped to 0..1 before averaging. These thresholds were checked on the saved player drawings, synthetic element drawings, and random-mark regressions during the $P+ migration. The existing combined ring-and-symbol quality check still applies. A seal matched to the grimoire as a whole uses that independent match instead; a genuinely empty ring still makes a shockwave.

Because $P+ can match a compound sign's fragments very well, a clear compact peripheral sign gets a preference over readings that tear it into smaller signs. This applies both when splitting a group and when joining already recognized fragments; it does not give the same preference to central sigils or large groups spanning the seal. Expansion's two arrowheads, for example, should remain Expansion instead of becoming two directional Regions.

Modifiers must span at least 0.06 of the ring radius. Normalization otherwise turns tiny erased remnants into complete signs, and the effect calculation gives even those marks half a modifier's strength. For weak sign matches (raw score below 0.65), the winning penalized score must beat the best different sign key by at least 0.025. Alternatives include candidates below the segmentation floor and group all poses of each key together. Clear matches keep accepting related shapes such as Column and Piercing; rough but distinctive signs also remain valid. These checks reject the whole spell with a size or ambiguity message, keeping the drawing editable for another recognition attempt.

The existing complexity cost (0.03 per modifier beyond four) also reduces the symbol quality used for acceptance. A cloud of unrelated marks must not become a spell by accumulating many mediocre matches, even inside a perfect ring.

**The seal as a whole.** The drawing is also compared with the grimoire's pages (`files/grimoire.lua`). Everything but the ring, in shares of its radius, is taken two ways: 128 points evenly along all the lines, as if it were one line - so it doesn't matter how many strokes the seal is drawn with (pages traced from the wiki's pictures are hundreds of pieces, a player draws long lines); and the lines themselves as dense points in a grid. The distance is how far, on average, the points of one drawing are from the lines of the other, both ways; each point has the direction of its line, and a line crossing at an angle counts as farther (otherwise in a complex seal everything is near everything). Three small turns and three sizes are tried (±6%: a hand-drawn ring is a bit bigger or smaller than the rest). Closer than the threshold - it is the wiki's seal (`spell.named`). If the drawing doesn't read sign by sign but looks like a grimoire page, the book still recognizes it (`seal_named_spell`).

The book reads a finished drawing with `read_spell` (`seal_spell.lua`; `tests/wiki_spells.py` reads its drawings the same way): a page known only as a whole first, then sign by sign. A copy of a page of signs whose signs don't read - a sign ambiguous or too small, a mark outside the ring (Spike, Carousel, Playland of Sand) - is that page when it is very close in shape (0.045) and clearly closer to it than to any other (`whole_sure`); the book's own seals come no nearer to a page than about 0.036, and they read anyway. A copy whose signs read as other symbols - the wiki draws some sigils its own way, Everflow's water reads as wind - is that page when it is within 0.035 and twice as close to it as to any other page (`seal_name`).

A page is left after the first, untransformed try only when it is that much farther than the closest page was untransformed: a copy turned a little is far from every page as drawn, and comparing it with the closest page's best turn used to pass over the right page when a near twin came before it in the grimoire. Two pages too alike to tell apart as a whole (Counterclock and Valance Leech Counterclock differ only in the middle) are told apart by their own parts - each page's points far from the other's lines: the drawing is the one whose own part lies on its lines (`twin_choice`).

## 2. Meaning: tree → spell (`compile_spell`)

Dictionary rules only, no geometry.

- **Element** - the element sigil. The same sigil twice adds strength; two different elements mix into a third (`DICTIONARY_MIXES`, the parameters are the average), three - "the ring will not hold". The size of the element sigils relative to the ring is the scale of the effect (canon).
- **Parameters** - force, focus, spread, range, lifetime: the sum of the deltas of the element and the signs, a sign's multiplied by its size.
- **Form** (carrier) - the vote of the form signs: column, dispersion, levitation; without them - "in place". An inverted sign votes for the opposite action (an inverted column strikes sideways - dispersion; inverted levitation cancels levitation and makes it heavy).
- **Behaviors** - signs add up: each adds its behavior with a weight (the sum of the signs' sizes; for piercing - the number of signs): `thrust` (column), `float` (levitation), `pull`/`push`, `crush`/`build`, `pierce`, `homing`, `grow`/`shrink`, `still`. The sign's type (`directional`, `semi`, `none`) decides whether it affects the direction and whether it can be inverted.
- **Spin** - the average sideways turn of the directional signs: from ~18° the `spin` behavior (the sign is the direction of rotation), less range.
- **Cost of complexity** - every sign beyond four lowers the precision.
- **Opposites** (`opposite` in `DICTIONARY_BEHAVIORS`) cancel each other: the difference of the weights remains, less than a third of a sign - nothing.
- **Compatibility**: an effect has a carrier (`carrier`: bolt, orb, hover, nova, cloud, ring, field), a behavior has a list of carriers it works on (`dictionary_behavior_works`); a seal with a way of manifesting of its own takes only the signs its way lists (`DICTIONARY_MANIFEST_SIGNS`: size, duration, count, harm, following the cursor). The other behaviors are dropped already at compile time - also from the signs drawn on top of a wiki's seal - so the summary promises nothing that doesn't happen, and `cast.lua` checks the same. Scattering needs an element with matter. `tests/test_sign_effects.py` casts every listed pair and checks that it changes what is made.
- **Convergence** compresses the element into its condensed variant: water - ice, wind - vacuum, fire - plasma, earth - stone, light - beam.
- **Direction and stability** - the vector sum of the directional signs and the symmetry of all signs. A balanced seal strikes straight at the aim point; a lopsided one sends the projectile in an arc and raises the chance of a misfire.
- **Empty ring** - a shockwave (canon).

- **Special sigils** set a way of manifesting of their own (`manifest`: Wind Underfoot - flight, Whorling Wind - a whirlwind, Sword - a blade...), **decorative** ones a look (`shape`: a sculpture of the element), **frames** a form or a way (rain, dome, pen, ribbon, puppet, flowers).
- **Regions signs** (wiki: Region Sign Demo): all one way - a column that way, facing each other - a ring, outwards - a wave, inwards - "within bounds", one outwards - a dash.
- **A wiki seal** is cast with its own spell: the book stores on the page the spell of the grimoire's page (`seal_page_data`) - with the meaning intended, not the one read by chance from the signs (`cast=` in `tools/grimoire.py`).

```
{ element, form, force, focus, spread, range, lifetime, heavy, tilt, push_x, push_y, stability, precision, floats, directed,
  shape, manifest, named, frame, layers, glaives, forbidden, span, behaviors = { key = weight }, subs = { spell }, links = { spell } }
```

Stored on the book's page as a `serialize_spell` string: `element=fire;...;b=thrust:2,pull:1.2`.

## 3. Manifestation: spell → cast (`cast.lua`)

A seal is not a spell card but a page of one of the witch's **books** (`files/books.lua`, section 4): this way the magic stays out of the vanilla spell list and "Progress". The pages are kept in the run's global variables (`book_var`: the Spellbook's `witch_notebook.seal_<i>_*`, the others' `witch_notebook.<book>.seal_<i>_*`), the active page's spell in `active_spell`. Book in hand, LMB - `spellbook.lua` calls `spellbook_use`, which reads the active page of the book in hand, with the book's delay between casts.

- The projectile - `dictionary_effect( element, form )`: the mod's own carriers (`carriers.lua` builds them at start from the element's look `DICTIONARY_LOOKS` and the templates `entities/templates/`): a bolt for the column, a floating orb for levitation, a field for a seal without signs, a cloud for dispersion; vanilla projectiles only for a unique mechanic (`DICTIONARY_OVERRIDES`). It appears from the hand, around the witch or at the cursor (clouds).
- Parameters → fields of the fired projectile: damage and blast radius (force), spread (focus and spread), speed and lifetime (range, lifetime), gravity (tilt, heaviness); the share of levitation among the columns - how slow and floating it is.
- Behaviors → components added to the projectile (`BEHAVIORS` in `cast.lua`, in the order of `DICTIONARY_BEHAVIORS`): homing - `HomingComponent`, piercing - `penetrate_entities` and copies, crushing - `penetrate_world` and `MagicConvertMaterialComponent` (rock → sand), inverted - sand → stone, expansion - the scale of the blast, of the damage areas and the sprite, stability - air friction without gravity, spin - the script `behaviors/spin.lua` (the projectile circles its path faster than it flies - spring loops along the aim), pulling - the script `behaviors/pull.lua`. A field takes force and lifetime into its area damage and its time; reflection turns enemies' projectiles back (`behaviors/reflect.lua`), Partition and inward Regions keep creatures on their side of its edge (`behaviors/bound.lua`); Detection puts a field, a wave or a cloud at the nearest enemy; a cloud with the Crosshair drifts after enemies (`behaviors/follow.lua`).
- The wave of Dispersion (`MANIFESTS.nova`, `effects/nova.lua`) takes Pulling (rushes in), Pushing and gusts (throws harder), Binding and Entwining (holds whom it strikes), Cooling (freezes them), Reflection, Expansion and Strengthening; what it does to the ground and liquids (crushing, binding sand, softening, purifying, clearing filth, freezing) is the game's conversion spreading with it (`wave_converts`). The ring of lights round the caster takes Expansion, Piercing (more lights), Stability and Strengthening. Any element has the ring; Lightning has the wave; black holes, lasers and ball lightnings fall as rain from the sky with the Sign of Rain. The Sign of Diamond is read and does nothing - the wiki doesn't know what it does.
- First the seal flashes in the air in front of the witch - in light, as it is drawn on the page.
- A seal with a way of manifesting of its own (`manifest`, `shape`) goes to `MANIFESTS` (`manifest.lua`): each manifestation creates one or more effect entities. An effect is an entity with parameters in `VariableStorageComponent` and the shared script `effects/run.lua`, which calls the mode of its kind: `aura` (on the witch), `zone` (in place), `mover` (moves by itself: sculptures, whirlwind, wave, decoys), `build` (structures of static physics bodies `entities/solid/*.xml`, drawn by `tools/make_gfx.py`), `light`, `portal`, `nova` (dispersion wave, blade), `orbit`, `held` (a held creature), `scry`, `repeat`, `knights`. The look is glowing cosmetic particles (`fx.lua`); sculptures are drawn after the template of their decorative sigil.
- Nested and linked seals are cast together with the main one (for a wiki seal with its own manifestation they are part of it).
- A forbidden seal brings the Stevari after a few seconds.
- No charges: the active page casts as often as you like, with a delay between casts.
- A misfire comes from precision and stability.
- "Straight" for a seal is the aim direction: in the manga a column strikes out of the paper, here - along the witch's gaze.

## 4. The books: inks, seal sheets, look

**Three books** (`files/books.lua`, `BOOKS`; wiki: Palm Quire, Magic Book). Each is an item of its own (`entities/spellbook.xml`, `palm_quire.xml`, `great_tome.xml`, the key in the `witch_book` variable) with its own pages, active seal and blank page:
- **Palm Quire** - round leaves 120 across, small seals only, casts quickly (18 frames). The manga's round notepad strapped to the hand.
- **Spellbook** - 180 pages, every run starts with it (30 frames).
- **Great Tome** - 240 pages, holds every seal, casts slowly (48 frames). A copy from the Tower of Tomes.
- **Test Book** (`entities/test_book.xml`, `files/test_book.lua`) - for testing only: nothing is drawn in it, its pages are every element (a sigil, a sigil with Convergence, two sigils mixed) in every form (Field, Column, Wave, Orb, Hanging, Rain, Ring) plain and with a pair of each sign that does something there, and every special sigil, frame and creature alone, with each element sigil and with the signs it takes - over 5000 seals, compiled from seal trees (`compile_spell`) and drawn from the templates when first shown. Its groups and items are in rows over the book, the item's forms under it; a click on a page makes it the active seal; it casts quickly (12 frames). Beside each page its notes (`files/spell_notes.lua`): what appears and where, its numbers (lifetime, speed, damage, blast, a field's radius and damage a second), what each sign does with its amount and what it needs to show (most act only on a creature they strike or on the ground and liquids), and the numbers the sign changed beside the plain seal. The same notes, told close, are written under any book for a drawn seal the mouse is over (in place of its one-line summary; beside the book, and then without the hints, where the screen is too short), with the inks it is drawn with (on average: blood's power jumps) and its misfire chance. The numbers are cast.lua's own: `spell_tuning` (what the parameters do to a carrier), `SIGN_AMOUNTS` (how much a sign does by its weight), `SIGN_CONVERTS`, manifest.lua's `MANIFEST_SCALE`, the carriers' `DICTIONARY_CARRIER_BASE`.

A book holds the seals of its size and smaller. A wiki seal's size (`grimoire.lua` `book`) comes from `tools/make_grimoire.py`: a sigil and at most 6 signs, nothing else, drawn simply (≤ 480 points) - the quire; 1400 points and more (Petrification, the Dragon's Labyrinth, Sasaran's Cloak...) - the tome; the rest - the Spellbook. A wiki seal drawn in a book too small for it doesn't awaken ("Too great for..."). The grimoire in a book shows the learned seals it holds. Drawn seals are kept in their book's page units; the grimoire's in the Spellbook's (`SEAL_PAGE_SIZE`), scaled to the page; the drawing flashing in the air is brought to them too.

The key in the `open_key` mod setting (B by default) opens the book in hand, else the one opened last (`witch_notebook.open_book`), else one the witch carries; a click with a held book that has no active seal opens that book too. The names over the book switch to the others they carry. Where they come from: the Spellbook - at the start; the quire - the Holy Mountain's shop, now and then (30% of mountains, in place of the lower row's second spell or wand) while the witch has none; the tome - the first boss the witch kills (`books_patch_bosses` gives every boss's entity file a death script, `boss_death.lua`), or the shop from the fourth mountain on (15%). A book picked up the first time (`book_pickup.lua`) becomes theirs (`book_owned`). The mod setting `books_at_spawn` (for testing, on for now) lays the quire and the tome beside the witch when a run starts; `test_book_at_spawn` lays the Test Book there (in a run begun before it was made, too).

**Inks** (`files/ink.lua`, `INKS`; wiki: Conjuring Ink, Magical Dye). Conjuring Ink in a flask with a dye becomes a new ink (`files/materials.xml`: a cell of dye and a cell of ink give two cells of the new one; in a flask the reactions run by themselves, shaking speeds them up): blood - Blood Ink, concentrated mana - Azure Ink (Azuremoon flowers), invisiblium - Clear Ink (Blushing Bride scales), gold - Golden Ink (Golden Blaze Wyrm scales), oil - Oil Ink (Roaming Scallop shells: does not run), acceleratium - Swift Ink. Water makes every ink run but Oil Ink.
- The book draws with the chosen ink (the bottles to the left of the book, `INK_VAR`); every stroke has its own ink (`page_inks`, a seal's field `inks`). The inks' shares along the length of the lines are written into the page's spell: `...;ink=blood:0.62,gold:0.2`.
- `cast.lua` (`ink_apply`) applies each ink by its share: blood - force and size, with a random swing, sometimes an extra sign and "boiling" (damage to the witch); azure - lifetime; gold - light on projectiles and effects (`effect_spawn` adds a `LightComponent`), the seal in the air shines long; clear - no flash of the seal, projectiles are barely visible, the Knights Moralis don't notice forbidden magic; swift - range and a shorter delay between casts (`ink_haste`). A wet witch (`WET` or in a liquid) casts weaker - the ink runs, except Oil Ink.

**Seal sheets** (`files/sheets.lua`). A wiki seal on a sheet lies around the world and is sold in the Holy Mountains; a picked-up sheet (`sheet_pickup.lua`) is pasted into a book as a page of its own - the book opened last if it holds the seal, else the first of the witch's books that does; a seal too great for all of them waits (`witch_notebook.waiting_sheets`) until the witch has a book big enough - and the seal counts as learned (`witch_notebook.learned`). A sheet is as big as the book its seal needs: a round leaf, a page, a folio.
- **A seal's tier** (`SHEET_TIER`) is set by hand by what it gives the witch, I-VII; the tiers are of different sizes (few at the top), the Great Tome's seals are IV and higher, a seal missing from the table is IV and fails `test_sheets_ranking`. The forbidden ones (the wiki's flag or the "Forbidden" section) separately, only in Hell and Heaven (`the_end.lua` is the script of both, `BiomeMapGetName` there is `victoryroom`), and drawn in blood.
- **A place's tier** (`sheet_tier_at`): by depth, the stretch between Holy Mountains is a tier (`TIER_DEPTHS`), parallel worlds and NG+ one higher. A sheet takes a seal of its tier, less often lower, rarely higher; not yet learned ones more often. The weights are per tier, shared by its seals, so a big tier comes up no more often than a small one. Deeper - dyed inks more often.
- Where from: flask spots (`spawnlist_append.lua`, 7% of `potion_spawnlist` spots), opened chests (`chest_append.lua`, 30%, in Hell and Heaven 55%), the chests and altars of Hell and Heaven (`the_end_append.lua`), the Holy Mountain shop (`temple_append.lua`, `sheet_shop_item`: a sheet or a flask of ink, from the third mountain sometimes dyed, replaces 10% of the shop's spells, at the height of its cards; wands are not replaced).
- The spawn scripts need the ranking, and loading the whole grimoire for them is costly: `sheets_create` at mod start writes it into the virtual file `files/sheet_list.lua` and draws the picture of every sheet.
- The "Wiki Grimoire" part shows only the learned seals - drawing, tier, description, to redraw them; the mod setting `full_grimoire` unlocks them all and allows casting straight from the grimoire (for testing).

**The books' look** (`files/book_gfx.lua`, `BOOK_LOOKS`): at mod start it draws every book's papers and covers. The Spellbook: fibrous paper in a ruled frame (a seal page, a text page, a pasted sheet with tape, a dark forbidden sheet, a starry endpaper with a bookplate, the title page), a red-brown leather cover with a gold frame and a stitched spine. The Great Tome: old vellum in a rubricated double frame with Noita's eyes, sheets held by wax seals, a marbled endpaper, a title page with an eye in a seal's ring and three eyes round it (the Brimmed Caps' eye, Noita's Kolmisilmä); black leather with iron corners, a gilt border of eyes, raised bands on the spine and a clasp. The Palm Quire: round leaves on a brass-rimmed pad, the lid above it with the manga's sigil (a triangle, lines from its corners to the rim) and a tab with a hole, two hinges, straps under the pad. Also the ribbon bookmarks, the bottles, soft shadows. Under the pages - their edges, thicker on the side with more pages. The bookmark lies on the active seal's page, otherwise its tail hangs out of the book on that side - a click on it opens the page.
- **Drawing** (`files/book_draw.lua`): the GUI calls, text that fits, soft shadows and a page lying flat as a surface; `notebook.lua` lays the open book out (`layout_spread`: two pages side by side, the words over and under them; `layout_quire`: the lid above, the pad below, the words in a column on the right) and draws its pages.
- **The open book** (`files/notebook.lua`): its state, the input and what is drawn around the pages. What doesn't need the open book's state stands apart: `book_store.lua` keeps a book's pages in the run's globals, `strokes.lua` is the text a drawing is saved as (the book in hand reads it back in `cast.lua`), `book_pages.lua` has the pen's kit, the hints and the front pages, `book_mouse.lua` the mouse on the gui and its calibration.
- **Turning a page** (`files/page_turn.lua`): the page is cut into strips across the way it turns - a book's into upright ones, it turns over its spine; the quire's round leaves into flat ones, they flip up over the hinge at their top onto the lid, where they lie showing their backs, the drawing faintly through them. The sheet is a curve: every point of the paper makes the same turn, but the closer to the hinge, the later (`sheet_angle`), so the sheet lifts at its edge, bends into an arc and lies down on the other side. The strips are drawn along the curve with perspective (raised paper is bigger) and light (turned away from the light - darker); where the paper leans over to the other side, its back shows - the next page. The page's content is drawn through a "surface" (`Flat`/`Curl`): the drawing's points, icons and text bend with the paper. The sheet's edge casts a soft shadow.

## Tests

- `tests/harness.py` is what the tests stand on: the lists of the mod's files (a file added to `init.lua` is added there), and the mod loaded three ways - what reads a drawing (`load_reader`), the books over stubs that record the gui and the globals (`load_mod`, `load_cast`), and a small fake world that runs (`world_runtime`). Tests take these from the harness, not from one another.
- `docs/fire-spells.md` records the fire spell audit. `tests/test_fire_spells.py` checks the floating bonfire, fire jet, two-stage burst, expanding fire ring, harmless cold flame/heat, repeated casts and incremental grimoire generation. `tests/render_fire_spells.py` renders their particle previews into `tests/output/`.
- `tests/test_spiraling_flame.py` checks the custom fire column's straight flight, walls, damage along its body, hit cooldown, old saved pages, Clear Ink and hand redraws of the wiki seal. `tests/render_spiraling_flame.py` records the actual Lua particle calls and writes an approximate animation and a wiki/page comparison into `tests/output/`. The game supplies the final particle glow and physical fire simulation.
- Reading: a generator builds a seal from the dictionary's templates (ring, element sigil, signs around, inverted), reading must return the intended tree.
- Meaning: from a known tree - the right spell.
- References: the seals of the wha-spell-simulator examples (Fire Column, Water Orb), hand-drawn - all the way through.
- `tests/debug_sigil.py` shows the tree and the spell for a drawing from the game; `--keep "name" element:form behaviors` saves the drawing into `tests/data/player_seals.json`, and the tests then check that it reads the same.
- `tests/bench_recognizer.py` - reading speed on 60 different seals; `--save`/`--compare` check that a speed-up doesn't change a single recognized sign.
- `tests/test_recognizer.py` - $P+ distances against an independent reference, curvature, pen lifts, degenerate input, agreement between full scoring and best-match selection, and alternative margins across distinct keys. Included in `tests/run_tests.py`.
- `tests/test_seal_structure.py` - hand-drawn stars, pen lifts in different orders and directions, small gaps, convex and disconnected outlines, the saved false Flickering Light loop, and small or ambiguous modifiers including rounded player drawings and the tome UI. Included in `tests/run_tests.py`.
- `tests/test_sign_effects.py` - every sign listed for a carrier or a way of manifesting changes what the spell makes, signs that do nothing are left out of a read seal, and what the signs on the wave, the field, the cloud and the ring do in the small world.

## Recognition speed

The $P+ implementation computes both directions' nearest points in one pairwise pass, using flat coordinate and angle arrays and taking square roots only for the chosen distances. It avoids a distance matrix and $P's greedy searches from multiple starting points. `recognizer_best` applies the same distance as full scoring, with stroke constraints, pose penalties, and a score floor. Upright poses come first to resolve exact ties consistently. `seal.lua` caches repeated readings of the same strokes in the same ring. The grimoire's whole-seal matching is independent of this recognizer.
